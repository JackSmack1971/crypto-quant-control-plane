# ENA Fundamental Input Recovery and Qualification

Run: ENA-PIT-DQ-20261023-03. Scope is dated fundamental input recovery and qualification. No confirmatory regression or return study was run.

## Decision

**Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE — confidence MEDIUM for the source-coverage findings; UNVERIFIABLE for complete point-in-time fundamental availability.** A historical USDe supply series has been recovered from Ethereum end-of-day contract state and passes structural checks. Current-backfill vendor fees/revenue histories are also complete through the pre-holdout boundary, but their original publication clocks and revision vintages are absent. Executed fee-funded buybacks and a defensible historical effective-float series remain unqualified. The evidence does not support a complete fundamental predictive test.

The frozen confirmatory design and holdout dates remain **2026-10-23 through 2028-10-22**, as instructed. No regression was fitted and no new lag or model choices were made. Holdout status: **exposed at the 2026-10-23 boundary**. Prior price and supply retrieval accessed boundary observations, and the DefiLlama payload contained the 2026-10-23 row; that date was excluded from pre-boundary coverage counts and calculations here, but exclusion does not reverse exposure. Do not describe this holdout as wholly untouched or use the boundary day as confirmatory evidence. Later holdout values were not examined in this work.

## USDe supply recovery details

The standalone CSV and the copy embedded in the downloaded calculation ZIP have identical SHA-256 hashes. The ZIP contains the source package and run metadata; its log reports 21 checks passed, including cross-endpoint matches and finality checks. Those are producer-reported checks. In this review the files were inspected and structurally reconciled, but the publisher’s code was not executed. The package also records HTTP 429 rate-limit failures from the Tenderly endpoint; the report says fallback/second-endpoint reads supplied matched values. This failure history is retained as a quality caveat.

The package’s companion cross-chain review reports, for a single Ethereum block on 2026-10-12, an OFT adapter holding 2,292,201,958.40 USDe and 2,292,201,862.94 USDe on other chains, with a 95.45-token difference. The review states the adapter balance backs cross-chain representation and Ethereum `totalSupply` already counts that backing. This supports using Ethereum canonical supply rather than adding other-chain displayed supplies, but it is only a one-date reconciliation and does not establish a historical bridge registry.

**OBSERVED:** the extracted contract state values and block identifiers in the recovered CSV. **UNVERIFIED:** whether a market participant could have observed each day’s final value immediately at the day boundary. **CALCULATED:** row, uniqueness, continuity and blank-value checks performed on the acquired files.

## Revenue and buyback interpretation

The Ethena governance update published October 13 reports Q3 monthly gross protocol fee income and says the governance vote approved prospective milestone-triggered fee-switch mechanics, with an initial stated $7.5B supply trigger. Recovered supply through 2026-10-12 is below that trigger. Approval does not establish implementation or completed purchases. Blockworks Advisory describes its own 705-day realized-data exercise, but the underlying daily panel is not available here. The archived DefiLlama files have filesystem timestamps of 2026-10-22 23:48 America/New_York (2026-10-23 03:48 UTC); filesystem timestamps are not exact source request or original publication clocks.

DefiLlama supplies daily histories with continuity within the returned payload but no vintage ledger in the acquired payload. DefiLlama's general metric definitions describe distinct fee, revenue and token-holder revenue concepts, but this acquisition did not verify a versioned field-level mapping for this endpoint. Those concepts do not automatically match Ethena/Blockworks gross protocol income, net revenue, beneficiary distributions, or payment dates. Do not merge these series or treat the daily API backfill as what investors knew at each historical timestamp.

An investor-token buyout/accelerated unlock is economically distinct from revenue-funded market purchases. Likewise, the announced fee-switch allocation is not itself an execution. The acquired evidence contains no transaction IDs, fill dates, venues, prices, costs, token amounts or verified daily ENA burn/treasury receipt series.

### Follow-up source checks: buyback ledger and ENA float

The fee-switch governance post recommends that public reporting include revenue/take rate, USD spent, ENA bought, average price, transaction IDs, burns, and buyback absorption relative to unlocks and effective float. It describes those as reporting requirements/recommendations and separates implementation into later governance work; that is a useful schema, not an execution ledger. A commenter says OAK has a list whose transaction IDs and amounts come from fills, but the accessible post does not include or link that list. It remains a specific lead to recover from OAK/Ethena, not evidence rows we can yet reconcile.

The post’s dashboard link resolves to Ethena’s [transparency dashboard](https://app.ethena.fi/dashboards/transparency), whose retrieved public page is labeled Proof of Reserves and displays supply/backing and custodian-attestation sections; it did not expose buyback fill rows in this retrieval. This does not prove no separate ledger exists. It means the publicly linked surface inspected here does not close the ledger gap.

The public [Blockworks Ethena token-transparency filing](https://blockworks.com/token-transparency/filing/ethena) is a better float source lead than a generic unlock calendar: it supplies initial allocation categories, vesting terms and a `Circulating Treatment` field, and reports facts as of 2026-08-25. It distinguishes Foundation treasury allocations as conditional and gives some transfer restriction terms, including StablecoinX-related locked ENA. It is not a daily freely-tradable float series: schedules do not prove a token moved or became liquid; the filing does not resolve beneficial ownership, exchange/custody/MM inventory, staking cooldown availability or all dated wallet balances. Treat it as one dated allocation/vesting vintage and a candidate component schema, not a complete float definition.

The report’s next float definition should therefore publish at least two reproducible measures rather than one opaque “circulating” scalar: (1) **unlocked supply** from the dated allocation/vesting schedule, reconciled to actual transfer-restricted balances where evidence exists; and (2) **estimated immediately tradable float**, subtracting only holdings with evidenced restrictions or control, while separately flagging staked/cooldown, foundation/treasury-controlled, bridged/escrowed, exchange/custody and market-maker balances with uncertainty ranges. Every label needs `valid_from`, `known_from`, source, revision and confidence; unknown beneficial ownership must remain an interval/unknown category rather than be forced into a label.

## Remaining gaps and next highest-value work

1. Preserve exact source snapshots and recover an as-known date/version history for the Ethena/Blockworks daily revenue panel; reconcile gross fees, retained revenue, and allocations by metric definition.
2. Extend the USDe daily chain series after 2026-10-12 only with finalized historical blocks and add per-day finality/first-index timestamps, RPC request provenance, chain identity and contract verification. Do not silently use later revised vendor supply.
3. Recover verified buyback executions from transaction/fill records and reconcile them to wallets, burns, treasury transfers and public issuer disclosures. Keep OTC investor repurchases separate.
4. Define effective float before collecting it; acquire timestamped wallet/vesting/custody/bridge evidence with labels’ valid-from, known-from and revision history.
